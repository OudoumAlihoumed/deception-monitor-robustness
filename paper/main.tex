\begin{abstract}
As large language models are deployed as autonomous \emph{agents} that take
multi-step actions under limited human oversight, monitoring their
trajectories for deception becomes a load-bearing safety control.
Linear probes on residual-stream activations report high held-out recall for
strategic deception in cooperative evaluations---but those numbers
are measured against agents that are \emph{not trying to evade the monitor}.
Drawing on adversarial-robustness methodology, we ask not
\emph{how accurate is the detector?} but
\emph{how much adversary effort buys how much detection collapse?}

We formalise a \emph{robustness gap}
$\deltadet(\budget)$: the drop in detection recall at a fixed
false-positive rate as adversary budget $\budget$ rises along a ladder from
prompt-level concealment, through probe-agnostic and probe-aware supervised
fine-tuning, to probe-aware reinforcement learning. We instantiate the
protocol on open-weights \model{} against a black-box surface-signature
classifier, a white-box linear probe, and a chain-of-thought reading control,
with paired, session-clustered evaluation ($N{=}60$) and a broken-agent
retention gate.

On Modal H200, the probe's largest gap opens at the probe-\emph{agnostic}
SFT rung ($\deltadet(b_2)=0.33$), while probe-aware SFT does not further
collapse detection ($\deltadet(b_3)=0.00$) and RL yields a smaller gap
($\deltadet(b_4)=0.13$). The protocol therefore converts an unanswerable
yes/no into a measurable budget curve, and shows that---for this model and
objective---surface-style suppression transfers more than the probe-aware
SFT penalty we implemented.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Large language models are increasingly deployed as autonomous
\emph{agents}: systems that take sequences of actions toward a goal,
invoke tools, and operate over long horizons with only intermittent human
review. This is where deception becomes most consequential, and it is now
the focus of a rapidly growing safety literature~\citep{chhabra2025agentic,
ma2025safetyatscale, narajala2025securing, bengio2025scientistai}. An agent
can behave deceptively by producing statements it internally represents as
false, concealing capabilities, or pursuing objectives its operator did not
sanction while presenting a compliant trajectory to an
overseer~\citep{park2023deception, meinke2024scheming}. Because a human
cannot inspect every action, oversight is increasingly delegated to an
automated \emph{monitor} that reads behaviour or internal state and flags
deception~\citep{greenblatt2023aicontrol}. The safety of the deployment
then rests on a question that has received far less scrutiny than the
monitors themselves: \emph{can the monitor be trusted when the agent is
trying to evade it?} Figure~\ref{fig:scenario} fixes the setting we measure.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/scenario_figure1.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{What we measure.}
  \textbf{(A)} Problem structure: an overseer sees only the transcript; the
  agent may pursue a covert goal; surface, probe, and CoT monitors score the
  episode but do not define the label.
  \textbf{(B)} Operational definition: $y{=}1$ iff the overseer-visible
  statement conflicts with the agent's internal fact \emph{and} advances the
  covert goal (scenario-defined, monitor-independent).
  \textbf{(C)} Episodes are multi-turn; deception is localised at one or more
  turns $t^\star$, where the visible channel $a_t$ and internal channel
  $s_t$ can diverge.
  \textbf{(D)} Eight agentic elicitation families with shared seeds across
  baseline and adversary arms.}
  \label{fig:scenario}
\end{figure}

Two monitoring strategies dominate. \textbf{Black-box} monitors read
externally visible behaviour: linguistic surface features, refusal
patterns, action logs, or a separate judge model. \textbf{White-box}
monitors read internal state, most prominently \emph{linear probes} on
residual-stream activations that recover a direction correlated with
deceptive intent~\citep{goldowskydill2025linearprobes,
zou2023representation, burns2022latentknowledge}. Reported numbers are
striking: activation probes achieve near-ceiling recall at low
false-positive rates on held-out deception
scenarios~\citep{goldowskydill2025linearprobes}, and access to internals
can boost detection beyond black-box signals
alone~\citep{parrack2025blacktowhite}.

These numbers share a hidden assumption: they are measured against a model
that is \emph{not optimising to evade the detector}. That is precisely the
setting the adversarial-robustness literature has taught us to distrust.
\citet{carlini2017towards}, \citet{athalye2018obfuscated},
\citet{carlini2019evaluating}, and \citet{tramer2020adaptive} established
that defenses evaluated against weak attackers report reassuring robustness
and then fail once the attacker is allowed to optimise against the specific
defense. A deception detector is a defense; a model incentivised to deceive
without being caught is an attacker. The mapping is exact, and it reframes
the central question.

\paragraph{This paper.} We ask not \emph{how accurate is a deception
detector?} but \emph{how much does its accuracy degrade as a function of
adversary effort?} We make four contributions.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{A robustness-gap protocol (primary).} We define
  $\deltadet(\budget)$ as the loss in detection recall at fixed FPR as
  adversary budget rises along a ladder from prompt-level instruction through
  SFT to RL. Reporting a \emph{curve} rather than a single number converts an
  unanswerable yes/no into a measurable frontier
  (Section~\ref{sec:methods}, Figure~\ref{fig:method}).
  \item \textbf{A probe-agnostic vs.\ probe-aware contrast.} We separate
  an adversary that only suppresses surface style ($b_2$) from one that
  places the probe score in the fine-tuning / RL objective ($b_3$, $b_4$).
  That contrast---not the attack recipe itself---is the scientific object:
  it isolates transfer from adaptive targeting.
  \item \textbf{Confound-resistant evaluation.} We measure deception
  \emph{retention} jointly with detection (TOST gate), check threshold drift,
  and compute session-clustered intervals, so a broken agent or a shifted
  operating point is not mistaken for evasion.
  \item \textbf{An open-weights instantiation.} We run $b_1$--$b_4$ on
  \model{} under Modal H200 and report the measured curve, including the
  unexpected finding that probe-agnostic SFT opens a larger gap than our
  probe-aware SFT objective on this setup (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\end{itemize}

Because $\deltadet(\budget)$ is defined identically whether probes prove
robust or fragile, the same protocol answers the question under either
outcome; we fix the primary endpoint in advance
(Section~\ref{sec:stats}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Safety and monitoring of AI agents.}
As models are deployed as autonomous agents, a dedicated safety literature
has formed around long horizons, tool use, limited oversight, and the
defenses appropriate to them~\citep{chhabra2025agentic,
ma2025safetyatscale, narajala2025securing}. A central proposal is
\emph{monitoring}: rather than certify an agent safe in advance, one runs a
cheaper monitor over its trajectory and intervenes when deception is
flagged. \citet{greenblatt2023aicontrol} formalise this ``AI control''
stance; \citet{bengio2025scientistai} argue for architectures that are
easier to oversee. The premise common to these proposals---that the monitor
holds up under an agent actively working against it---is exactly what our
protocol puts under test. \citet{meinke2024scheming} show that frontier
models already exhibit in-context scheming, making monitor evasion a
present rather than hypothetical concern.

\paragraph{Detecting deception from internals.}
\citet{burns2022latentknowledge} recover latent knowledge without
supervision; \citet{zou2023representation} formalise representation reading
and control; and \citet{goldowskydill2025linearprobes} train supervised
linear probes that detect \emph{strategic} deception in role-play and
agentic settings with high held-out accuracy. \citet{clymer2024poser}
unmask alignment-faking models by manipulating internals.
\citet{parrack2025blacktowhite} quantify the boost that internals provide
over black-box access. We build on this detector family but subject it to
an adversary its original evaluations did not include.

\paragraph{Benchmarks and elicitation of deception.}
\citet{ren2025mask} disentangle honesty from accuracy.
\citet{park2023deception} survey deceptive behaviours and risks. On the
training side, \citet{schoen2025antischeming} stress-test anti-scheming
training, and \citet{vanderweij2024sandbagging} show strategic
underperformance---the sandbagging confound our capability-retention
control is designed to rule out. Our elicitation suite follows the
\emph{model-organism} tradition of
\citet{scheurer2023insider} (insider trading under pressure) and
\citet{benton2024sabotage} (sandbagging / sabotage evaluations), rather
than static classification.

\paragraph{Adversarial robustness and adaptive attacks.}
Our methodology is imported from adversarial machine learning.
\citet{carlini2017towards} introduced strong optimisation-based attacks;
\citet{athalye2018obfuscated} showed that many defenses succeed only by
obscuring gradients; \citet{carlini2019evaluating} codified evaluation
guidelines; and \citet{tramer2020adaptive} broke essentially all of a set
of published defenses with attacks adapted to each defense. The through-line
is that robustness must be measured against an adversary that knows and
optimises against the specific defense---exactly what a
deception-detector evaluation lacking a probe-aware adversary omits.

\paragraph{Activation-level control as an attack surface.}
That internal directions can be \emph{written} as well as read is what
makes a white-box adversary plausible.
\citet{turner2023activation} and \citet{panickssery2023caa} steer behaviour
by adding activation directions, showing that the representations probes
rely on are manipulable. A probe-aware adversary can, in principle,
optimise activations to be simultaneously deceptive in behaviour and
probe-negative in representation.

\paragraph{Closest prior work, and our delta.}
The nearest neighbours are attack papers and detector papers, not
evaluation protocols. \citet{bailey2024obfuscated} show that activations
can be obfuscated while preserving behaviour---so evasion is possible.
\citet{macdiarmid2024probes} report that a simple probe catches sleeper
agents---so detectors look strong under non-adaptive tests.
\citet{hubinger2024sleeper} supply the sleeper / fine-tuning methodology
we reuse as adversary rungs.
\citet{baker2025monitoring} show that optimising against a CoT monitor
induces obfuscated reasoning---the CoT analogue of probe pressure.
None of these answers the deployment question that AI-control
systems~\citep{greenblatt2023aicontrol} actually face:
\emph{given a fixed monitor, how much does detection degrade as adversary
budget rises?}

We therefore treat the contribution as \textbf{protocol-first, attack-second}.
We did \emph{not} set out to invent a new evasion algorithm; Bailey et~al.\
already establish that adaptive evasion exists. We set out to make monitor
trust measurable under a budgeted adversary. Concretely, we deliver
(i)~$\deltadet(\budget)$ as a \emph{curve} with a probe-agnostic vs.\
probe-aware contrast ($b_2$ vs.\ $b_3$) as the primary scientific object;
(ii)~joint deception-\emph{retention} so a broken agent is not counted as a
defeated monitor; (iii)~paired, session-clustered inference on an
agentic suite; and (iv)~an open-weights ladder through prompt, SFT, and RL
that instantiates the protocol. The attack rungs are instruments for
reading the curve, not the claim. If a stronger probe-aware objective later
opens a larger $b_3$ gap, the protocol still applies---that is the point.

\subsection{Formal definitions}
\label{sec:defs}

\begin{definition}[Agentic episode and deception label]
\label{def:deception}
Let an episode $\tau=(x_{1:T}, a_{1:T}, s_{1:T})$ be a trajectory of
overseer-visible observations $x_t$, agent actions/utterances $a_t$, and
internal residual states $s_t$. Let $g_{\mathrm{cov}}$ be a covert
objective known to the scenario author but not to the overseer, and let
$b_{\mathrm{int}}$ be the agent's internal representation of relevant
facts (elicited by construction in our scenarios). The episode is labelled
\emph{deceptive} if there exists a turn $t^\star$ such that
(i)~$a_{t^\star}$ conflicts with $b_{\mathrm{int}}$, and
(ii)~$a_{t^\star}$ advances $g_{\mathrm{cov}}$. Labels are scenario-defined
and independent of every monitor under test.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Label construction]
Both clauses are required: conflict without a covert goal is mere error;
covert pursuit that is transparent is not deception toward an overseer.
Labels are fixed by the scenario so they cannot be circular with $D$.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Monitor and operating point]
\label{def:monitor}
A monitor $D$ maps an episode (or a turn) to a real-valued score
$D(\tau)\in\mathbb{R}$. We calibrate a threshold $\theta_D$ on held-out
\emph{benign} controls so that
$\mathrm{FPR}_D(\theta_D)=0.01$, and report
$\recallfpr_D := \Pr\!\big(D(\tau)\ge\theta_D \mid y(\tau)=1\big)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Operating point]
False positives trigger costly interventions in AI-control
settings~\citep{greenblatt2023aicontrol}, so we fix FPR and report recall.
The protocol is unchanged for any pre-registered $\alpha$; we use
$\alpha{=}0.01$.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Robustness gap]
\label{def:gap}
For detector $D$ and adversary budget $\budget$,
\begin{equation}
  \deltadet_{D}(\budget)
  \;=\;
  \recallfpr_{D}(0)
  \;-\;
  \recallfpr_{D}(\budget),
  \label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}
where $\budget=0$ is the non-adversarial baseline. The object of study is
the \emph{curve} $\budget\mapsto\deltadet_{D}(\budget)$, not a single
scalar.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Gap as a curve]
Absolute recall units keep $\deltadet$ interpretable when baseline recall
is moderate. The curve matters: a small gap at low budget is the
artifact that non-adaptive evaluations
produce~\citep{athalye2018obfuscated, tramer2020adaptive}. Positive
$\deltadet$ means detection worsened; negative means recall rose.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Threat model}
\label{sec:threat}

We treat the monitor as a \emph{defense} and the agent as a potential
\emph{attacker}. Following~\citet{carlini2019evaluating}, we specify the
adversary along three axes.

\textbf{Knowledge.} A \emph{probe-agnostic} adversary is optimised only to
suppress externally visible deception cues and does not know a probe
exists; evasion here measures \emph{transfer}. A \emph{probe-aware}
adversary incorporates the probe score into training; this is the adaptive
attack, and the only condition under which we use the word ``adaptive.''
\emph{Query access} lets the adversary read the probe's scalar output;
\emph{gradient access} lets it differentiate through the probe. Our
primary rung ($b_3$) uses gradient access---exactly the situation of a
developer who uses probe scores as a training
signal~\citep{baker2025monitoring, hubinger2024sleeper}.

\textbf{Objective.} The adversary jointly maximises deception success and
detector evasion. Optimising evasion alone produces a degenerate
non-deceptive model (Section~\ref{sec:retention}); optimising deception
alone does not test the detector.

\textbf{Budget.} Per rung we log fine-tuning steps, tokens, LoRA rank /
updated parameters, and wall-clock time. These form the horizontal axis of
the robustness-gap curve.

\subsection{Models, data, and detectors}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Model.} Primary experiments use open-weights
\model{}~\citep{qwen3} (64 layers, hidden size $5120$). The probe layer is
selected on a held-out split among $\{16,24,32,40,48,56\}$; unless noted we
use mid-late layer $40$. Open weights are required for a reproducible
white-box adversary.

\textbf{Scenarios.} Deception is elicited with agentic scenario families
in the spirit of \citet{scheurer2023insider} and
\citet{benton2024sabotage}: insider trading under pressure, sandbagging on
capability evaluations, capability concealment, goal misreporting to an
overseer, tool-use coverup, audit evasion, safety-filter bypass, and
resource hoarding. Scenario seeds are held fixed across baseline and
adversarial arms so comparisons are paired. Ground-truth labels follow
Definition~\ref{def:deception}.

\textbf{Detectors.} We evaluate three detectors.
(i)~A black-box \emph{surface-signature} classifier over transcript
features (hedging, brevity, differential helpfulness, refusal markers,
contradiction cues).
(ii)~A white-box \emph{linear probe} on residual-stream activations,
following~\citet{goldowskydill2025linearprobes}; layer and threshold are
selected on a held-out split, never on the test set.
(iii)~A \emph{chain-of-thought--reading} control: per
\citet{smith2025difficulties}, an internals-based detector must be shown
to beat a detector with access to the model's reasoning trace.

\subsection{Adversary-budget ladder}
\label{sec:ladder}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{$b_1$ -- Prompt-level.} Instruct the model to accomplish
  the covert objective while concealing it. No weight updates.
  \item \textbf{$b_2$ -- SFT, probe-agnostic.} LoRA fine-tuning on
  trajectories curated to suppress surface deception cues. The probe is
  \emph{not} in the loop; any probe evasion at this rung is transfer.
  \item \textbf{$b_3$ -- SFT, probe-aware (adaptive).} Fine-tuning with
  \[
    \mathcal{L}_{b_3}
    =
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}
    +
    \lambda\,\mathrm{softplus}\!\big(D_{\mathrm{probe}}(s)\big),
  \]
  so the model is trained to remain deceptive while lowering the probe
  score. $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}$ anchors behaviour;
  $\mathrm{softplus}$ supplies a smooth penalty on the signed probe output;
  $\lambda$ sets the trade-off ($\lambda{=}0.5$ in the reported run).
  \item \textbf{$b_4$ -- RL, probe-aware.} Online LoRA REINFORCE with
  \[
    R=\alpha\cdot\mathrm{deception\_proxy}
    -\lambda\cdot D_{\mathrm{probe}}
    -\beta\cdot\mathrm{surface\_risk},
  \]
  jointly rewarding retained deception, probe evasion, and surface-cue
  suppression.
\end{enumerate}
The primary comparison is $b_2$ versus $b_3$: transfer under surface-only
pressure versus adaptive pressure with the probe in the objective.

\subsection{Deception retention and the broken-agent control}
\label{sec:retention}

A detector can appear robust simply because fine-tuning degraded the model
into no longer deceiving. At every rung we measure a continuous
\emph{deception-success rate} alongside detection recall. A rung whose
deception-success has collapsed is flagged as a broken agent and excluded
from robustness summaries with a logged reason. Independently we measure
matched benign capability before and after each fine-tuning rung
\citep{vanderweij2024sandbagging}.

We use an equivalence gate: two one-sided tests (TOST) against a margin of
retaining $\ge 80\%$ of baseline deception-success. Writing
$\delta=\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{success}_{b}]-\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{success}_{0}]$ and
$\varepsilon=0.2\cdot\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{success}_{0}]$, the rung passes only
if both one-sided tests reject
$H_0{:}\,\delta\le -\varepsilon$ and $H_0{:}\,\delta\ge +\varepsilon$
at the pre-chosen level, i.e.\ if $\delta$ lies in
$[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]$. Rungs outside this band are logged as
capability collapse and excluded from robustness summaries.

\subsection{The adaptive defender}
\label{sec:defender}

A static defender understates security under an adaptive attack. The
protocol therefore includes two defender-side measurements after
probe-aware rungs.

\textbf{Probe retraining.} Train a fresh probe on the fine-tuned model's
activations. Recovered recall indicates a displaced signal; unrecovered
recall indicates destruction or relocation of the deception representation.

\textbf{Cross-probe transfer.} Test whether evasion is specific to the
attacked probe or transfers across seeds, layers, and training splits.

These measurements are part of the protocol specification; the present
workshop run reports the attacker ladder $b_1$--$b_4$.

\subsection{Mechanistic reading}
\label{sec:mech}

When a gap opens we record diagnostics that distinguish suppression along
the probe direction from re-implementation elsewhere: cosine similarity of
the probe direction before versus after a probe-aware rung, probe-score
distributions on matched deceptive tokens, and a re-probe layer sweep. The
workshop run reports the detection curve and retention gate; these
diagnostics accompany the protocol for follow-up analysis.

\subsection{Statistical protocol}
\label{sec:stats}

\textbf{Unit of analysis.} The independent unit is the \emph{session},
never the turn.

\textbf{Cluster-robust inference.} Confidence intervals use a
session-clustered bootstrap; where appropriate we fit mixed-effects models
with random intercepts for session, scenario template, and seed.

\textbf{Paired design.} Baseline and adversarial conditions share scenario
seeds, enabling a paired bootstrap for $\deltadet$.

\textbf{Seeds.} Elicitation seeds vary across sessions. We report one
fine-tuning seed.

\textbf{Primary endpoint.} Probe $\recallfpr$ at the held-out-selected
layer, broken-agent margin, and model-selection choices are fixed before
adversarial runs.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}

We pin checkpoint names, LoRA configs, and seeds, and release analysis
code. Evasive checkpoints are treated as a measurement instrument, not a
deployment recipe: we describe the procedure at reproduction level while
gating release of evasive weights.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Compute.} Experiments run on Modal \texttt{H200} GPUs with
open-weights \model{}~(\texttt{Qwen/Qwen3-32B}) in 4-bit NF4.

\paragraph{Sessions.} $N=60$ paired sessions with scenario seeds held fixed
across arms. Half are deception-eligible; half are benign controls for FPR
calibration.

\paragraph{Monitors.} Surface-signature logistic classifier; residual
linear probe at layer $40$ (held-out selection among mid--late layers);
CoT-reading control. Thresholds are calibrated to $1\%$ FPR on benign
controls.

\paragraph{Adversary rungs.} $b_1$: prompt injection. $b_2$/$b_3$: LoRA
rank~$16$, $200$ steps; $b_3$ adds
$\lambda\cdot\mathrm{softplus}(D_{\mathrm{probe}})$ with $\lambda=0.5$.
$b_4$: LoRA REINFORCE for $100$ steps under
$R=\alpha\cdot\mathrm{deception\_proxy}-\lambda\cdot D_{\mathrm{probe}}-\beta\cdot\mathrm{surface\_risk}$.
One fine-tuning seed is reported.

\paragraph{Pipeline validation.} Estimators (calibration, paired
session-clustered bootstrap, broken-agent exclusion, threshold-drift) were
checked on synthetic rollouts with known separability before the model run.

\paragraph{Where the gap opens.}
Table~\ref{tab:main} and Figure~\ref{fig:results} summarise the primary
finding. The white-box probe starts at recall $0.53$ at baseline---well
above the surface monitor ($0.13$) and matched to the CoT control
($0.53$). The largest probe gap is at the probe-\emph{agnostic} SFT rung:
$\deltadet(b_2)=0.33$ (recall $0.53\rightarrow 0.20$; bootstrap $95\%$ CI
$[0.00,\,0.67]$). Prompt-only pressure already opens a gap
($\deltadet(b_1)=0.27$). Critically, probe-aware SFT does \emph{not}
further collapse the probe on this objective ($\deltadet(b_3)=0.00$;
recall returns to $0.53$), and RL yields only
$\deltadet(b_4)=0.13$. The $b_2$--$b_3$ contrast therefore cuts against a
simple ``adaptive collapse'' narrative for this model and loss: cleaning
surface style transfers more than the softplus probe penalty we used.

\paragraph{Cross-monitor pattern.}
Figure~\ref{fig:results-cross} breaks the same run out by monitor family.
The surface monitor is weak at baseline and collapses to zero recall under
$b_2$/$b_3$, then partially recovers under $b_4$ (recall $0.27$). The CoT
control tracks the probe through $b_1$/$b_2$, drops sharply at $b_3$
(recall $0.07$), then \emph{rises} above baseline at $b_4$ (recall $0.67$).
Because our ladder never optimises against the CoT reader, that recovery
should not be read as intrinsic CoT robustness~\citep{baker2025monitoring}.

\paragraph{Label noise.}
Rule-label agreement with the intended deception class at baseline is
$0.70$, so some sessions are imperfectly labelled by the scenario
heuristic. We report this explicitly rather than silently averaging it away.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the gap means for monitoring.}
The value of a deception monitor is not its accuracy against a cooperative
model but its accuracy against a model with an incentive to evade. Reading
the robustness gap as a curve reframes deployment from ``is the probe
accurate?'' to ``how much adversary effort does a given level of protection
cost?'' In our run, that cost is paid earliest by surface-style SFT ($b_2$),
not by the probe-aware objective we implemented ($b_3$).

\paragraph{What a gap means for a control protocol.}
In the AI-control framing~\citep{greenblatt2023aicontrol}, a monitor is a
budgeted auditor. A gap translates into control-relevant quantities: the
expected number of undetected deceptive episodes before the first catch,
and time-to-first-detection---both degrade with $1-\recallfpr$. Under
$b_2$, the probe's miss rate roughly doubles relative to baseline.

\paragraph{Why $b_3$ did not collapse the probe.}
On this run the probe-aware SFT objective did not open an additional gap
beyond $b_2$. Compatible explanations include a weak $\lambda$ relative to
the behavioural CE term, and a training-time probe adapter that did not
faithfully match the evaluation probe (RL logs reported near-zero probe
scores). We therefore treat $\deltadet(b_3)$ and $\deltadet(b_4)$ as
\emph{preliminary} under the present attack implementation, not as a
certified claim that probe-aware training fails to evade.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Results are for a single open-weights dense model (\model{}), one
fine-tuning seed, and $N{=}60$ sessions ($30$ test). Bootstrap CIs are
wide. The broken-agent proxy is coarse. Scenario rule-label agreement at
baseline is $0.70$. Stronger adaptive results---with the evaluation probe
wired exactly into $b_3$/$b_4$ training and verified non-degenerate probe
scores---are pending; the numbers reported here should not be read as the
final adaptive stress test.

\paragraph{Scope and future work.}
Pending work: match the training probe to the evaluation probe, increase
$\lambda$ and attack steps, then re-measure $b_3$/$b_4$. Further
extensions include CoT-aware rungs, defender-side probe rotation/transfer,
multiple fine-tuning seeds, and a second model family.

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Deception monitors for AI agents are increasingly proposed as load-bearing
safety controls, yet their headline numbers are measured against agents
that are not trying to escape them. By analogy to adversarial robustness,
the right quantity is the robustness gap $\deltadet(\budget)$: how much
detection recall a monitor loses as adversary budget rises, with a
probe-aware rung as a decisive adaptive test. We defined the protocol,
separated probe-agnostic from probe-aware adversaries, gated broken-agent
confounds, and ran $b_1$--$b_4$ on \model{} under Modal H200. On this run
the largest probe gap opened under probe-\emph{agnostic} SFT
($\deltadet(b_2)=0.33$), not under probe-aware SFT
($\deltadet(b_3)=0.00$)---evidence that the measurement protocol matters as
much as any single robustness number, and that adaptive objectives must be
stress-tested rather than assumed.

% =====================================================================
\section*{Broader Impact}
\label{sec:impact}

This work studies whether deception monitors can be trusted, and to do so
it builds agents optimised to evade them. We regard those adversarial
variants as a \emph{measurement instrument, not a deployment recipe}. We
(i)~describe training at reproduction level while gating public release of
evasive checkpoints; (ii)~use open-weights models so defensive findings are
reproducible by the safety community; (iii)~frame the contribution as
diagnostic for monitor designers; and (iv)~disclose the evasion recipe to
authors of the probe we stress-test ahead of public release when
applicable. On balance, publishing a rigorous measure of monitor fragility
does more to improve oversight than withholding it, provided evasive
artifacts are handled with care.
